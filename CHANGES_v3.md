# What changed from the national version 2 to version 3, and why

Version 2 (5 September 2026) covered all 50 states + DC with straight-line ×1.2 distances between county population centers and land-area weights for districts. Version 3 (9 September 2026) keeps the parts that were already right — the registry pull and QC, site-to-county assignment, NCI center list, member data, the Connecticut fix — and replaces the two coarse methods with the ones the Texas version had already proved, then adds the features below.

## Method changes

| Weakness in v2 | Fix in v3 | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Straight-line ×1.2 as distance | Shortest routes on the Census TIGER 2025 primary/secondary road network, all 51 states, 1.09 M nodes (same method as Texas v2, stored as sparse arrays to fit in 3 GB); validated on 38 city pairs, mean error 3.1% | Road / straight-line ratio to the nearest broad menu: median 1.20, 10th–90th pct 1.10–1.38; the ×1.2 rule was too low for 1,481 of 3,025 counties. National % beyond 60 miles of a broad menu 37.0 → 38.8 |
| District weights by land area | Tract population 55+ weights (84,396 tracts, 89,397 tract–district records) | Mean absolute change in "% beyond 60 mi of a broad menu" 3.5 points across 436 districts; median 0; 42 districts move >10 points. Largest: CA-25 7.3 → 100.0, CA-23 2.4 → 87.7, CA-41 0 → 53.9, WA-6 29.3 → 72.5, FL-8 21.6 → 62.4 — all districts holding the far half of a big metro county whose *centroid* is near the menu (Riverside and San Bernardino counties each host ~55 trials, so Coachella Valley residents are ~100 road-miles from Orange County's population center, not 0) |
| "% in a zero-trial county" as lead metric | "% with fewer than 20 (or 0) trials within 60 road-miles" | 20.9% → 12.7% (<20) and 4.4% (none): the county-boundary statistic overstated isolation, as the ZCTA analysis had suggested |
| Alaska / Hawaii scored with straight-line distances to the mainland | Labelled "no road connection"; counted in a separate no-road percentage | 35 counties, 0.7% of residents 55+; no longer produce 1,700-mile "distances" |
| Connecticut: one site row dropped via a planning-region code; no ACS household data | City-fallback layer uses 2021 county shapes; covariates rebuilt from tract tables on legacy counties; county pop 55+ now ACS 2023 tract sums (was 2021 PEP) | Every CT number is now on the same geography and vintage as every other state |
| Tract population not reconciled | Reconciled exactly to all 3,143 counties; Suffolk and Ulster NY raked (ACS 2023 publishes 371 of Suffolk's 385 2020 tracts) | 98,596,429 residents 55+ at tract, county, state and national level |

## Things the build found that the spec did not expect
- **Indiana is present** in State Cancer Profiles 2018–2022; **Kansas** is the state that does not release county data. The source also suppresses 9 counties with fewer than 16 cases in the period.
- ACS 2023 publishes fewer tracts for Suffolk County NY than the 2020 geography; 18,235 residents 55+ are in no tract row. Raked to the county total (factor 1.038, logged).
- The registry's explicit phase value "NA" is read as missing by pandas' defaults; 255 eligible trials are "not phased" (device, surgery, radiation), now labelled as such.
- Roughly a dozen supportive-care / non-oncology trials survive the inherited rule screen (surfaced by the classifier's unclassified bucket); left in for comparability and listed for review.

## Deferred, with reasons
- **Tier 4 with the intended candidate universe.** `www.facs.org` and `ncorp.cancer.gov` were added to the sandbox allowlist but the running container did not pick the change up, so the CoC + NCORP crawl (`fetch_candidates_coc_ncorp.py`) is written as a contract and plan, not run. The full computation is built and tested on the registry stand-in; substituting the intended list is one command.
- **Oral-agent classification** — explicitly out of scope; fields remain in `trials.csv`, unused.
- **Independent (blind) check of the cancer-type classifier** — the 200-trial sample with classifier output is left for a reader who did not build it.

## 2026-09-29 · classification fix
- **Mucosal melanoma no longer reaches organ filters.** `Anorectal Melanoma` matched the colorectal pattern (`rectal`) and `Sinonasal Melanoma` the head-and-neck pattern, so NCT05546827 (preoperative radiotherapy and immunotherapy for sinonasal and anorectal mucosal melanoma, MD Anderson) was listed under the Colorectal filter as a basket trial. A precedence rule now keeps only `melanoma_skin` for any condition string that names melanoma (the same treatment uveal melanoma already had). The published data were patched for this one trial (it is now a single-type melanoma trial); the precomputed per-cancer-type breakdown cards for areas within 60 road-miles of Houston carry a one-trial overcount for colorectal and head & neck until the next full refresh. No other trial was affected: a scan of every condition string that names melanoma together with another organ found only this one and the already-handled uveal case.

## 2026-09-30 · classification audit and release gate
- **Systematic audit of the cancer-type classifier** after the mucosal-melanoma case: every condition string among the eligible trials was checked for a category matched by a stem inside a longer word, for non-neoplastic wording, and for melanoma strings reaching another category. Fixed: *gallbladder* cancer no longer reaches the Bladder filter (`(?<!gall)bladder`); *pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma* are neuroendocrine, not Brain & CNS (the stem "glio"); *craniopharyngioma* is Brain & CNS, not Head & neck ("pharyn"); *ganglioneuroblastoma* and *esthesioneuroblastoma* go to their own categories; *extramammary Paget disease* is a skin cancer, not breast ("mammary"); *oral leukoplakia / erythroplakia / dysplasia* are Head & neck, not Leukemia ("leuk"); *mucosal melanoma of the head and neck* is melanoma; *endobronchial metastases* is a metastatic site. 23 of 4,173 eligible trials change category (17 of the 3,915 open to 55+); each was checked by hand. Published data patched for those trials; the basket share moves from 28.7% to 28.5%.
- **`audit_cancer_types.py`** now runs in every refresh right after classification and lists the strings that could mislead the classifier; strings already reviewed are in `audit_allowlist.txt` (74 today). A new one stops the refresh until a person looks (`qc_cancer_type_audit.md`).
- **`release_gate.py`** runs last: the classification audit, plausibility bounds on every headline number, drift limits against the previous weekly snapshot (a jump means a broken pull, not a real change), and payload consistency (trial, county, district and state counts; no missing district figure). If any check fails, nothing is published and GitHub Actions reports the failure (`RELEASE_GATE.md`).

## 2026-10-05 · pre-review fixes: road network, site locations, NCI centers, pages (method version 2)
A page-by-page and file-by-file check before the map went to mentors found wrong figures and broken features. Everything below is fixed in the scripts, so each refresh reproduces it; nothing was patched by hand in `docs/`.

**Figures that were wrong, and why**
- **Road distances.** The national graph had nodes only at crossings and line ends, so a place could join the network miles away, at the dead end of a one-way carriageway, and be routed 60–300 miles to a hospital in its own town (parts of Tucson, Kalispell, Spokane, Pueblo, Duluth, Desert Hot Springs). `build_roads_us.py` now puts a node at least every 400 m, joins only junction nodes within 500 m (never the two banks of a river, never the two ends of one bend), links a highway's dead end to another road within 5 km when no short route exists, and lets no link cross a county line or coastal water. About 194,000 residents 55+ had a recruiting site within 20 straight-line miles but none within 60 road-miles; 22,000 do now, and those are cut off by water with no bridge (south Whidbey Island, the New York shore of Lake Champlain). Ferries are still not modelled.
- **Where sites are.** A ZIP code larger than 100 square miles has its centroid in open country: 16 miles from Billings, 15 from Great Falls, 18 from Durango, 180 from Fairbanks. Sites in such ZIPs (2,334 site rows) are now placed at their city (`tier4_covering.SiteLocator`).
- **Which county a site is in.** A ZIP that spans counties was given to the county with most of its land. It now goes to the county where most of its residents 55+ live unless the site's own city is in the first (`data/ref/zcta_county_parts.csv`, `metrics_us.assign_sites`): 291 site rows moved, among them Munson Medical Center (Leelanau → Grand Traverse County), the University of Virginia (Albemarle → Charlottesville), Fairbanks Memorial (Yukon-Koyukuk → Fairbanks North Star), Carilion (Roanoke County → Roanoke city) and Athens, GA (Jackson → Clarke). The ZIP finder and the map's ZIP search use the same rule.
- **NCI centers.** St. Jude Children's Research Hospital was the "nearest NCI center" of 145 counties (1.7 million residents 55+), who cannot be treated there; it stays on the list but is no longer a distance target, so Memphis is now 199 road-miles from the nearest center (Vanderbilt), not 11. Centers are located at their hospital's ZIP code instead of the city centroid, which was 5–13 miles off for 14 of them (Mayo Clinic in Phoenix and Jacksonville, USC Norris, the University of Arizona, the University of Kansas). The pages say "65 centers at 67 locations" from the data (Mayo Clinic's three campuses are one designation).
- **Facility names** lost their closing bracket ("…(TRIO-US"); 481 are repaired. Names that are only a sponsor's site code ("014", "USA02-0") are now flagged as not identified (331 → 417).

## 2026-10-05 · validation pass: telemedicine rows, missed cancer trials, mistyped ZIPs, city names (method version 3)
A second, longer check of the published site against outside sources: 40 cities against ClinicalTrials.gov, 417 ZIP-finder searches (84,544 result cards) against an independent recount, every state, district and county panel and brief (7,262 views) against the source files, 5.9 million road distances, the member list and cosponsors against today's official files, the NCI list against cancer.gov.
