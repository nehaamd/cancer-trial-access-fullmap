# US Cancer Trial Access by County and Congressional District — version 3

**What this is.** For every US county (3,143), congressional district (435 + DC) and state: how many recruiting cancer treatment trials are within reach of residents aged 55+, how far they must drive to a broad menu of trials or to an NCI-designated cancer center, which trials those are, how many people are diagnosed with cancer there, and what household barriers (vehicle, broadband, insurance, poverty) sit alongside the distance. Version 3 brings the national map up to the rigour of the Texas road-network version: shortest highway routes instead of straight-line ×1.2, census-tract population weights instead of land-area weights, and adds trial lists, filters, a cancer-incidence layer and household context. Every page shows the date of the registry data it is built from (also in `data/raw/fetch_log.json`), earlier pulls are kept as snapshots, and the difference is shown in the app's Recent changes tab.

Web app: `docs/` (static, deployable to GitHub Pages as-is; `index.html` map with Trials / Sites / Compare counties / Burden vs access / What changed tabs, `find.html` a ZIP-code lookup for patients and navigators (recruiting trials ranked by estimated distance, with links to the study records and to the NCI, Blood Cancer United and American Cancer Society navigation services; nothing entered leaves the browser), `brief.html` printable briefs — viewable by county, congressional district or state, with phase / sponsor / cancer-type filters that recompute county counts and the access figures and maps for districts, states and the nation at census-tract level —, `trials.html` per-county trial lists, `sites.html` the Tier 4 research prototype, plus `data.js`, `geo.js`, `tier4.js`, and `tracts60.bin` — a 6 MB census-tract file the page loads once, only when a filter is applied to a national, state or district view, so that filtered access figures are recomputed at tract level in the browser; d3 loads from cdnjs with a local fallback).

## Headline results (residents 55+, trials accepting some adults aged 55 or older, road miles from each census tract's population center)
Registry data as of 5 October 2026: 4,001 listed trials at about 9,600 registry-listed facilities (2,450 distinct site locations).
- **12.0% of Americans 55+ have fewer than 20 recruiting cancer treatment trials within 60 road-miles of home; 4.2% have none.** (21.0% live in a county with no trial — a county-boundary statistic that overstates isolation and is not the lead number.)
- **38.1% live more than 60 road-miles from a broad menu** (a county with 100 or more trials); 18.4% more than 120; 0.7% (Alaska, Hawaii) have no road connection to one at all.
- **43.7% live more than 60 road-miles from an NCI-designated cancer center that treats adults** (65 centers at 67 locations); the median resident 55+ is 48 road-miles from one; 20.8% are more than 120.
- 377 counties host at least a limited menu (20 or more trials), 83 of them a broad menu; **15 states have no broad-menu county**; 73 congressional districts have every resident 55+ beyond 60 road-miles of one.
- Road distance / straight-line distance to the nearest broad menu: median 1.18 (10th–90th percentile 1.09–1.34); the common ×1.2 convention is too low for 1,275 of 3,025 counties.
- **Rural vs urban (USDA Rural-Urban Continuum Codes, 2023): 43.1% of residents 55+ in nonmetro counties (codes 4–9) have fewer than 20 recruiting trials within 60 road-miles, vs 6.1% in metro counties; 20.0% vs 1.2% have none; median road distance to an NCI-designated center 115 vs 35 miles.** Nonmetro counties hold 16.0% of residents 55+.
- **Need vs access: 497 counties (4,341,298 residents 55+, about 88,182 new cancer diagnoses a year) have above-median all-sites incidence AND at least half of residents 55+ with fewer than 20 trials within 60 road-miles** — a cross-tabulation of the two published measures, never a combined score (the page lets the reader change the cancer site, the access criterion and the threshold).
- Router check: 38 published city-pair driving distances, mean difference 3.1%; 23 local routes (short in-town trips and water crossings), all within tolerance. Reference distances are approximate; see VALIDATION_v3.md.

## Method in plain language
1. **Trials.** Ask ClinicalTrials.gov for every interventional study that is recruiting, has treatment as its purpose, matches an oncology condition, and lists a US site. The registry matches whole words and its own disease coding, so the search also asks by name for cancers it files outside its neoplasm category or has not coded (myelodysplastic syndromes, myelofibrosis, adenocarcinomas, glioblastoma, metastases; `config.CONDITION_QUERY`). Drop the non-cancer studies the broad search lets in (a study is kept only if its conditions or title name a cancer; wording such as "tumor necrosis factor" or "non-malignant" does not count; `qc_us.is_onc`) and the trials that cannot enroll anyone 55 or older.
2. **Where the trials are.** Put each recruiting site in a county by ZIP code (3-digit ZIP, then city name as fallbacks; Connecticut on its eight legacy counties). Two kinds of registry row are treated differently. A row that names a telemedicine or virtual service instead of a clinic is not a place: it gets no county, is not drawn and is not a site within any distance (one fully decentralized study lists "Ohio State University-Telemedicine" in 107 cities; `config.VIRTUAL_SITE_PATTERN`). Such trials are shown separately, labelled Telemedicine, on the panels, the trial lists, the briefs and the ZIP finder for the states they name. A row whose ZIP is a typing error is placed where the registry's other rows list that facility; the ZIP is judged an error only when the same named facility is listed repeatedly near the stated city and never near the ZIP ("University of Iowa, Iowa City, 52252"; `metrics_us.zip_typos`). Otherwise the ZIP wins over the city name, because sponsors often write the parent organisation's city for a satellite clinic. A ZIP that spans counties goes to the county holding most of its land, unless most of its residents 55+ live in another of its counties and the site's own city is not known to be in the first (`data/ref/zcta_county_parts.csv`): ZIP 49684 is mostly Leelanau County by land, but Munson Medical Center and two-thirds of the ZIP's older residents are in Traverse City, Grand Traverse County. A trial "is in" a county if it has a recruiting site there; a facility is a distinct registry facility name in a county after removing sponsor site codes. ≥100 trials = *broad menu*, ≥20 = *limited menu* (ASCO's 2024 thresholds).
3. **What the trials are.** Each trial is assigned one *primary* cancer category from its registry condition text, or the explicit "multiple cancer types / basket" bucket when it names several cancers or only generic terms (about three trials in ten; the share is on the map's methods panel). Filtering by a named cancer type also returns basket/multi-cancer trials that name that cancer among others (well over a hundred trials reach the Colorectal filter this way, for example) — the filter checks every category a trial's conditions name, not just its primary label. Phase and lead-sponsor class come from the registry's structured fields.
4. **Where the people are.** Population 55+ for each of 84,396 census tracts (ACS 2023 5-year) and each tract's population center; tract sums reconcile exactly to every county.
5. **Roads.** Build a highway network from the Census Bureau's primary and secondary roads for all 50 states + DC, with a node at every crossing and at least every 400 m, and compute shortest routes. The Census file has no ramps and a highway's designation often stops short of the next highway, so junctions within 500 m are joined and a dead end within 5 km of another road is linked across the gap — never across a county line or coastal water (`build_roads_us.py` explains each rule). Checked on every refresh against published city-pair driving distances and against a table of local routes: short in-town trips, which must not be routed the long way round, and water crossings, which must not be shortened (`out_adult55/road_validation.csv`, `road_local_checks.csv`). Ferries are not modelled.
6. **Access.** Each recruiting site is located at its ZIP centroid (state-checked; city centroid or county center as fallbacks). A ZIP code larger than 100 square miles — a rural hub with the town in one corner — has its centroid in open country (16 miles from Billings, 15 from Great Falls, 180 from Fairbanks), so a site in such a ZIP is placed at the centroid of its city instead. For each tract and county: distinct trials with a recruiting site within 30/60/120 road-miles of the population center; road miles (and indicative hours) to the nearest broad menu, limited menu and NCI-designated center that treats adults (each located at its hospital's ZIP code; a center with several campuses is measured to each; St. Jude Children's Research Hospital is listed but is not a distance target). Alaska and the Hawaiian islands have no road connection to a broad menu and are labelled, not scored.
7. **Districts.** Average tract values within each 119th-Congress district, weighting by population 55+ (tracts split by a boundary apportioned by land area).
8. **Cancer burden.** Age-adjusted incidence, 95% CI and average annual cases by county and cancer site from NCI/CDC State Cancer Profiles (NPCR + SEER, 2018–2022), with every county labelled published / small numbers (<16 cases a year) / suppressed by the source / not available (Kansas, by state law).
9. **Household context.** Households without a vehicle, with broadband, uninsured, and poverty rate from ACS 2023 5-year tables, shown as four separate layers — never combined into an index. Counties show county values; districts, states and the nation are summed from census-tract tables (`build_tract_context.py`; tracts split by a district line counted by share). Checked against the Census Bureau's own congressional-district tables: median difference 0.1 percentage point for poverty and 0.0 for households without a vehicle; the few larger gaps are in states whose lines changed between the 118th and 119th Congress (GA, LA, NC). District cancer cases are each county's State Cancer Profiles count shared out by where its residents 55+ live, and are labelled as an estimate; the district incidence rate remains a residents-55+-weighted mean of county rates, because incidence is not published below the county.
11. **Rural / urban.** USDA ERS Rural-Urban Continuum Codes (2023) for every county — 1–3 metro, 4–9 nonmetro by urban population and adjacency. Connecticut's 2023 codes are published for its planning regions, so its eight legacy counties carry their 2013 codes (flagged). The same tract-weighted access measures are then grouped by the code of each tract's county, for the nation and each state; district panes show the share of residents 55+ in nonmetro counties.
12. **Burden vs access.** For each county, the share of residents 55+ (weighted over its tracts) with fewer than 20 / no recruiting trials within 60 road-miles — for all trials and for each named cancer type (multi-label rule) — set against the county's age-adjusted incidence for the chosen site. The page cross-tabulates the two (incidence above / below the median of published counties × access share above / below a threshold the reader sets; default: at least half with fewer than 20, or, with a cancer chosen, none for that cancer), maps the four cells, and lists the high-burden / low-access counties by annual diagnoses. Never combined into a score.
13. **Cosponsors.** Sponsor and cosponsor lists for the tracked bill (Clinical Trial Modernization Act, H.R. 3521 and S. 4440) from govinfo.gov BILLSTATUS XML (or api.congress.gov with a key), joined to districts and Senate seats through legislators-current.json; withdrawn cosponsors excluded; former members flagged.
14. **ZIP lookup (find.html).** ZIP → Census ZCTA population center → straight-line distance × 1.20 (the median road / straight-line ratio measured on this network) to every recruiting-site location; trials at sites within the chosen radius, filtered by cancer type (basket trials that name it included), age, phase and sponsor, nearest first. Labelled as an estimate; the map pages use full routing.
10. **Where would an additional site help (research prototype).** Take existing trial-capable facilities that host no listed trial today and whose county has fewer than 20; for each, count the residents 55+ who have no listed trial within 60 road-miles today but would if a trial opened there; rank them, and pick greedily (each pick removes the people it covers). The intended candidate universe is Commission on Cancer-accredited programs plus NCORP affiliates; until those lists are pulled, the prototype runs on a stand-in (every US facility that hosted an oncology treatment trial started 2016 or later) and is labelled as such.

## Before anything is published: the audit and the release gate
Every refresh runs two checks that stop it, with nothing published, if they fail (GitHub Actions then reports the failure):
- `audit_cancer_types.py` (right after classification) lists every registry condition string that could pull a trial into the wrong cancer type — a category stem matched inside a longer word ("anorectal melanoma", "gallbladder", "paraganglioma"), non-neoplastic wording, or a melanoma string reaching another category. Strings already reviewed are in `audit_allowlist.txt`; a new one holds the refresh until someone reads `qc_cancer_type_audit.md`, fixes the pattern or accepts the string (`--accept`).
- `release_gate.py` (last) checks plausibility bounds on every headline number, drift against the previous snapshot (a jump means a broken pull, not a real change), the road network (every local route check within tolerance; few residents with a site nearby in a straight line but none by road), and payload consistency; see `RELEASE_GATE.md`. A deliberate change of method is registered in `config.METHOD_CHANGES`: the drift check is skipped once across it, and the Recent changes tab marks the rows calculated before it.

Weekly review after a green refresh, about ten minutes: read the Actions log; open `qc_cancer_type_audit.md` for anything marked NEW; open the map's "Recent changes" tab; check two districts you know well (for example your own and TX-11); glance at the national numbers on the findings page. Corrections are logged in `CHANGES_v3.md`.

## Weaknesses that remain
- **Site locations are ZIP-code centroids** (city centroids for ZIP codes larger than 100 square miles), not street addresses; a few registry rows carry a ZIP that disagrees with the stated city and follow the ZIP. Menu distances (nearest county with 20+/100+ trials) remain county-level by definition.
- **Router validation uses approximate published reference distances, not an independently-fetched routing benchmark.** The mean difference is a real computation but the city-pair and local-route comparison distances are approximate reference values, not re-fetched from a routing service using the router's own coordinates (no routing API is reachable from the build environment). The page says so; treat the figure as directional, not certified.
- **Trials, not eligibility.** Counts pool all cancer types unless filtered; the cancer-type filter is regex-based and about three in ten trials sit in the multi-cancer bucket by design. Filters apply to county counts and lists and to the *access* figure (share with fewer than 20 / none / average count within 60 road-miles) for the nation, states and districts, recomputed live from census tracts; distance-to-menu and distance-to-NCI-center figures depend on the full trial set and are shown tagged as unfiltered.
- **Highway-only network.** Local roads are not in the graph; the last mile is a straight-line access edge (median about 0.6 mi for tracts) and drive-times use fixed per-class speeds, so hours are indicative. Grade-separated crossings without interchanges may create false connections (small effect on long routes), a dead-end highway is linked to the nearest other road within 5 km as if a local street joined them, and ferries are not modelled, so islands and peninsulas without a bridge are scored by the road route or as having no road connection.
- **County population centers stand in for trial sites** when pooling trials within a distance band (as in ASCO's method and the Texas build).
- **Registry limits.** "Recruiting" lags real enrollment; facility names are free text; ~12 supportive-care / non-oncology trials survive the rule screen (listed for review).
- **Districts are those of the 119th Congress (the maps used in the November 2024 election).** Several states have redrawn for 2026; the Census has not published 120th-Congress relationship files.
- **Cancer burden** is a different period (2018–2022) and geography vintage than the registry; Kansas is absent by state law; small counties are flagged, not hidden.
- **Snapshot.** One day's registry and one day's member list (dates on the page); re-pull before distributing. The NCI-designated center list (the NCI list in `qc_us.py`, last compared with cancer.gov in June 2026) should be re-checked against cancer.gov — designations change (e.g. the Medical College of Wisconsin Cancer Center was seeking designation as of mid-2025).
- **Tier 4 is a research prototype.** It runs on a registry-derived stand-in candidate universe, so it can only name places already in ClinicalTrials.gov; roughly one candidate in ten carries a sponsor placeholder name instead of a facility name (flagged †). The intended CoC + NCORP universe needs `fetch_candidates_coc_ncorp.py` to be run once those domains resolve; `validate_v3.py` keeps one check failing until then.
- **Rural / urban codes** are county-level and categorical; a metro county's outlying tracts can be rural in character (the ACS `pct_rural_2020` field in `county_covariates.csv` is the block-level alternative, unused). Connecticut's legacy counties carry 2013 codes.
- **Burden vs access** compares different periods (incidence 2018–2022; trials on the pull date) and the incidence sites map only approximately onto the trial cancer-type categories (e.g. Oral Cavity & Pharynx → Head & neck; Thyroid → Neuroendocrine & endocrine); counties whose incidence rests on fewer than 16 annual cases are hatched. The threshold and criterion are choices, stated on the page, not findings.
- **Cosponsorship** is a snapshot of the Library of Congress feed on the fetch date and is one signal of a member's position, not a vote; re-run `fetch_cosponsors.py` before a meeting.
- **ZIP lookup distances** are straight-line × 1.20 from the ZCTA center, not routed; ZIPs without a ZCTA (PO-box-only) are not found. The list is of trials, not of trials a patient is eligible for; the page says so and routes people to the NCI, Blood Cancer United and American Cancer Society navigation services.
- **Not in this version** (explicitly out of scope): oral-agent classification (fields present in `trials.csv`, unused — left out until it can be validated), real-time enrollment, eligibility matching, trend-over-time.
